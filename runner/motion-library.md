# Tiny Soho motion library

What the Studio Mac picks from when it shapes a reel's look and storyboard, and what the Claude.ai brainstorm
project pitches from. Built on 8 Oct 2026 from the 232 entries on prompt-motion.com and the sections on
motionin.design, filtered for Tiny Soho: calm, premium, editorial, never kiddish, 9:16, drawn in code
(three.js and canvas) with AI images only for the cast and print-style objects.

Each reel takes one **style** (its visual language), one **treatment** (the world it is staged in) and a few
**moves** and **transitions**. Mix freely, but one idea and one signature move per scene.

## 1. Styles

### Fits Tiny Soho
| Style | Use it for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic type | Hooks, myth-busting, lists | The brand's default voice: Fraunces, one rose italic word per line, words placed with weight |
| Swiss style | Myths, studies, numbers | Strict grid, one colour plus ink, big numerals, hairline rules. See Blue editorial showreel |
| Risograph | Rough weeks, confessions, Halloween | Two inks slightly misregistered, halftone grain, paper warmth. See Risograph studio intro |
| Paper collage | Lists, myth-busting | Reel 01. Cut-outs with white edges and soft shadows, a new paper colour per scene |
| Flat 2D | Simple stories with the cast | Flat shapes and soft gradients; keep the palette muted |
| Line art | Explaining, tender moments | A single-weight line that draws itself on |
| Watercolor | Tender, bedtime, grandparents | Soft washes that bloom in; slow |
| Pencil sketch | Toddler's-eye stories, notes | Sketchbook pages, graphite lines drawing on. See Sketchbook animals come alive |
| Cross-stitch | Grandparents, home, tradition | Stitches filling in row by row; works for words too |
| Woodcut / engraving | Myths, history, "studies show" | Old-print illustrations with one accent colour painted over. See Engraving-style ad |
| Origami | Transformations, before and after | Paper folds into the next shape |
| Mid-century | Nostalgia, grandparents, routines | Warm muted colours, simple geometric illustration |
| Infographic | Numbers, schedules, sleep hours | Bars, clocks and timelines that draw themselves, labelled in Inter |
| Split-flap | Countdowns, clocks, schedules | Departure-board letters flipping into place; ideal for the clock change |

### Use with care
Each can work for one reel; keep it calm and on palette.

| Style | The risk |
|---|---|
| Bauhaus | Primary colours read loud; use the shapes, keep brand colours |
| Art Deco | Can feel like a luxury ad; fine for one special reel |
| Art Nouveau | Ornament can bury the message; use it for frames only |
| Blueprint | Cold; good for "how sleep works", not for feelings |
| Bento grid | Reads as an app promo; good for lists of 4–6 |
| Isometric | Good for a room or nursery (see Enxovaly baby app promo); can look like a tech product |
| Clay 3D | Easily kiddish; needs AI images, not code |
| Stained glass | Heavy; one emotional reel at most |
| Topographic | Abstract; works as a texture only |
| Film noir | Moody; only for a deliberate joke ("the case of the missing nap") |
| Cel shading | Reads as cartoon; keep characters still and composed |
| Manga | Strong genre signal; one reel at most |
| Pop art | Loud halftone and outlines; use the halftone, not the shouting |
| Tilt-shift | Needs real photography of a scene |
| Memphis | Shapes and squiggles read kiddish fast |
| Skeuomorphism | Only for one object (a real-looking notebook or clock), not a whole look |

### Off-brand: don't use
Glassmorphism, Y2K chrome, Synthwave, Glitch, Particles, Neobrutalism, Vaporwave, Pixel art, Low poly, ASCII art,
Frutiger Aero, Op art, Neumorphism, Aurora gradient, Graffiti, Sci-fi HUD, Psychedelic, Neon sign, Grunge,
Surrealism. They are loud, techy or chaotic, and would make @tinysoho look like a different account.

## 3. Moves (inside a scene)
- **Mask-line rise:** text rises out of a hairline mask, word by word, landing on the spoken word.
- **Letter stagger:** each letter scales, unblurs and rises on a short stagger. Use for the hook only.
- **Marker draw-on:** handwriting and arrows draw themselves with stroke-dashoffset; a hand-drawn circle closes round the key word.
- **Stamp:** a verdict (MYTH, TRUE, IT DEPENDS) lands with a spring and a firm sound.
- **Meter or bar:** a gauge needle or bar draws to a value, overshoots slightly, settles.
- **Card stack gather:** a fanned row of cards gathers into one hero card with the rest stacked behind (motionin "Lens Bloom").
- **Hairline connect:** a thin line draws from a label to a number and the number lights on contact.
- **Light change:** the sky or room light shifts (dusk to night, lamp on), carrying time without a cut.
- **Light spill:** a doorway or window opens and warm light falls across the floor.
- **Split-flap flip:** letters or numbers flip into place, one column at a time.
- **Stitch or brush fill:** a shape fills in row by row (cross-stitch) or blooms (watercolour).
- **Count-up:** a number counts up with tabular figures, then holds.
- **Words build structure:** a heavy word becomes part of the layout that later lines stand on.
- **Breathing idle:** held objects drift a few pixels on slow, unrelated periods so a still frame stays alive.

## 4. Transitions (between scenes)
- **Object carry:** one element connects every scene: a dot, a candy, a clock becomes the next scene's hero ([Orange dot motion system](https://prompt-motion.com/ultimaxbt-bbebf5)). Tiny Soho's default.
- **Shape flood:** a shape grows to fill the frame and contracts into the next scene.
- **Iris:** a circle closes on a detail (an eye, a window) and opens on the next scene.
- **Page turn:** the page lifts and the next one is underneath (sketchbook, diary, storybook).
- **Fold:** the paper folds into the next shape (origami, a blanket).
- **Curtain or sign drop:** the stage curtain or a hanging sign drops in to change the scene (shadow theatre).
- **Line wipe:** one full-width line crosses the frame; above it the old scene, below it the new (motionin "Pixels").
- **Light switch:** the frame flips from light to dark, or from day to night, on a beat.
- **Push into texture:** the camera pushes into a stamp, a stitch or a paper grain, and out into the next scene.

## 5. Rules the best references follow
- One continuous take feels premium: scenes are made out of the previous one. No crossfades, blur-ins or 3D flips.
- No dead time: something changes on every beat, but only one thing is the focus.
- One saturated accent in an otherwise quiet frame. Two saturated colours make a palette, not a focal point.
- Springs with a tiny overshoot at most. Never bouncy, never wiggle or hop.
- A sound for every on-screen hit; master at -14 LUFS.
- Text sits where it can be read: no tiny labels in the corners, nothing important under Instagram's bottom fifth.
- For a loop, make the last frame identical to the first.
- Avoid what reads as AI-made or template: particle bursts, glows, gradients on everything, corner frame text,
  random transitions not connected to anything on screen.
